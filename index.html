About

About me

I am a Master's student in Computer Science and Technology at the Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), advised by Prof. Pietro S. Oliveto. My research lies in the theoretical analysis of randomized search heuristics and evolutionary algorithms — understanding, rigorously, how long these algorithms take to find good solutions and why.

My current work focuses on hyper-heuristics: algorithms that combine and adapt a set of simpler heuristics. I design them and establish the first provable runtime guarantees for their performance. I am seeking Ph.D. positions in theoretical computer science, especially in randomized search heuristics and stochastic optimization.

Runtime Analysis of Complete Hyper-Heuristics

Jan 2025 – Jun 2026

Primary contributor · advised by Prof. Pietro S. Oliveto

  • Designed Fast Adaptive MAHHβ, a complete hyper-heuristic combining heuristic selection and move acceptance; led the analysis, experiments, and first manuscript.
  • Established the first rigorous runtime analysis of a complete hyper-heuristic using both operators, with guarantees across pseudo-Boolean benchmark functions.
  • Key results: Θ(n log n) on OneMax, O(n³/d² + n log n) on Cliffd, and O(k log n · C(n,k)) on Jumpk.
Outcome: accepted for publication in IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation (TEVC), 2026.

Runtime Analysis of Reinforcement Learning Hyper-Heuristics

Sep 2025 – Present

Primary contributor · with Zhenyu Wang and Peizhou Wu, advised by Prof. Pietro S. Oliveto

  • Proved the first rigorous positive result for a reinforcement-learning hyper-heuristic from the literature, showing RLHH optimizes LeadingOnes in the best possible expected runtime achievable with RLS₁ and RLS₂.
  • Proposed and analyzed an adaptive penalty mechanism replacing a fixed, problem-dependent penalty with online recalibration; proved optimal runtimes on LeadingOnes and OneMax.
  • Key results: (1+ln 2)/4 · n² + o(n²) on LeadingOnes; (1+o(1)) n ln n on OneMax — the first hyper-heuristic matching the unary unbiased black-box complexity of OneMax up to its leading constant.
Outcomes: published in PPSN 2026; follow-up under review at AAAI 2027.
